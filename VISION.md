# The experience

Drift is a quiet companion: a little rocket, an endless starscape, and gentle ambient sound. It should leave room for reading, working, or simply watching.

The rocket flies forward without an endpoint. A long opening journey visits planetary systems, stars and remnants, nebulae, and galaxies; subsequent cycles vary the route. There are no scores, destinations to complete, or required interactions once the journey begins. Controls stay at the edge and dim when idle.

The camera occasionally changes angle over a slow transition, with long periods of steady framing between moves. Manual input takes priority. The rocket always makes forward progress while following gentle lateral curves, with its heading and banking derived from that path.

The soundtrack should support studying: low-volume sustained harmonies, occasional soft keys, long attacks and releases, little bass, and no percussion. It should remain easy to ignore.

The look is a dark navy universe with a ceramic rocket, cool planetary light, and restrained lettering. Objects, stars, and sound are generated in the browser.

Aim for astronomical visual cues: atmospheric limb light, textured surfaces, dark planetary nights, stellar granulation, diffuse gas, dense stellar populations, and an opaque black-hole shadow. The journey deliberately compresses real cosmic scales and translates otherwise invisible emissions into an illustrative visible palette. It must not claim to be a physical simulation or an exhaustive astronomical catalogue.

Long sessions matter. Geometry stays bounded, hidden tabs stop rendering and suspend audio, and reduced-motion preferences start flight paused. Added complexity should earn its cost through a calmer or more convincing experience.
